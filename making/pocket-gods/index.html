a page from the making of / Native prototype

Pocket Gods

A small planetary adventure with a native campaign and local co-op systems.

the curiositythe buildthe practice

01 / the question behind it

How can a suit make flying between tiny planets feel playful, powerful and easy to read?

Pocket Gods is a native Godot flight prototype with three small planets and the Stillwater Rift. Procedural suit, planet and creature geometry supports a solo expedition, plasma and beam tools, upgrades and host/join co-op.

02 / from idea to something playable

how the pieces came together.

  1. 01

    Define a readable flight vocabulary

    Movement separates rising, descending, boosting and braking. Mouse aim controls the direction of play, while flight bindings can be changed in the suit settings.

  2. 02

    Build worlds around the suit

    The three planets and Stillwater Rift provide places to travel, fight and interact. Planet surfaces, plants, creatures and suit geometry are created for the project rather than assembled from a downloaded character pack.

  3. 03

    Connect weapons and progression

    Plasma fire and a sustained beam give the suit two different combat actions. The upgrade screen and interaction control keep the expedition’s progression close to the movement loop.

  4. 04

    Package a playable expedition

    The Windows export runs without a Godot installation. Two-player host/join uses a reachable UDP host; LAN and Internet sessions have different network requirements.

03 / the skills in the work

what this helped me practice.

The work develops 3D movement, procedural modeling and the relationship between combat controls and camera readability. Packaging the game also exercises native export, input settings and network-session design.

  • Godot
  • 3D flight controls
  • Procedural modeling
  • Co-op systems
  • Native export

a note on the process.

Flight needs more than speed: a dependable brake, clear vertical controls and distinct weapon behavior give movement a shape. A portfolio sequence should show that control loop before the scenery.

where the project stands

Playable Windows prototype; visual development, physical-controller testing and public-Internet co-op testing remain in progress.
