a page from the making of / Native prototype

Slime Rangers

A cartoon expedition with a blaster, vacuum, scanner, co-op extraction, and a ship to return to.

the curiositythe buildthe practice

01 / the question behind it

What if an alien expedition felt like a cartoon toy box, with every tool giving a different way to explore?

Slime Rangers is a native Godot exploration prototype built around a blaster, vacuum and scanner. Co-op extraction and a return ship connect the expedition’s tools to a clear way home.

02 / from idea to something playable

how the pieces came together.

  1. 01

    Give the expedition different verbs

    The blaster, vacuum and scanner create a broader interaction vocabulary than a single weapon. Each tool is part of how the player reads and acts on the alien world.

  2. 02

    Stage the encounter around something to protect

    The recorded outpost encounter brings incoming slimes toward a coolant rig. The first-person blaster, hit feedback and objective HUD keep the threat and the task in the same view.

  3. 03

    Let the outing change the next action

    The field-tech menu offers three upgrades, including Decoy Bloom. The recorded sequence returns from that choice to the outpost, connecting progression to the active encounter. Co-op extraction and the ship form the wider expedition premise.

03 / the skills in the work

what this helped me practice.

The work explores tool design, 3D world readability and how several interactions can fit one expedition loop. The cartoon presentation also makes consistent silhouettes and visual feedback part of the design problem.

  • Godot
  • Exploration systems
  • Tool interaction design
  • Co-op prototyping
  • Visual readability

a note on the process.

A tool is easier to understand when the objective gives it a purpose. In this slice, the coolant rig anchors the encounter and the field-tech choice gives a reason to reconsider the next action.

where the project stands
