Moonbite

A small gardener on a spherical moon. Plant, water, explore, and survive what grows after dark.

the curiositythe buildthe practice

01 / the question behind it

Could a whole little moon feel like a garden you want to come back to?

Moonbite pairs a real Three.js spherical world with illustrated characters and crops. The simulation is separated from rendering, input and audio, so planting, watering, manual combat and saved gardens can share the same rules.

02 / from idea to something playable

how the pieces came together.

  1. 01

    Make the surface behave like a world

    Movement follows the moon’s curvature and transports the control frame around it. Characters, soil, plants and attack warnings stay rooted to that same surface, including the poles and far side.

  2. 02

    Build a small care loop

    A planted bed stores its moisture and growth. Watering chooses a real nearby crop, shows its moisture ring and spends only the water the plant needs; dry soil visibly darkens when it becomes wet.

  3. 03

    Give actions a clear moment of contact

    Machete cuts have wind-up, one directional hit and recovery. Walking follows actual travel, while planting settles into the soil and watering uses a timed pour, keeping feedback connected to the action.

  4. 04

    Protect the place between visits

    Home and survival journeys have independent checkpoints. Plants, resources, positions and mining scars survive a return home, and older save formats are migrated instead of silently discarding the garden.

03 / the skills in the work

what this helped me practice.

The project develops spherical movement, state persistence and readable action animation. It also brings together sprite integration, procedural scenery and original Web Audio sound.

  • Three.js
  • Spherical movement
  • Game state & saves
  • Sprite animation
  • Procedural audio

a note on the process.

The useful detail is often the smallest one: soil color, a grounded foot or a correctly timed cut tells the player what the rules are doing. Keeping those signals tied to the simulation makes the garden easier to understand.

where the project stands

An illustrated farming-survival prototype; the linked hosted build is private and the newer local work remains in development.
