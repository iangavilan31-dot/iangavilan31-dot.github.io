Mirefall

A flooded swamp, a colossal moth, and a third-person boss encounter made with procedural graphics.

the curiositythe buildthe practice

01 / the question behind it

How can a huge boss’s scale become a mechanic instead of only a camera effect?

Mirefall is a third-person Three.js action encounter in a flooded swamp. Procedural meshes, textures, sound and music support a three-phase moth-deity fight with readable warnings, a poise break and phase checkpoints.

02 / from idea to something playable

how the pieces came together.

  1. 01

    Turn size into a vulnerability rule

    The deity’s head is out of reach during normal play. Attacking its legs breaks poise and brings the head down for a stronger damage window.

  2. 02

    Build anticipation into every attack

    Ground shapes, eye cues and distinct sounds signal attacks before impact. Recovery windows give the player an understandable chance to respond and punish.

  3. 03

    Escalate the same arena

    Later phases add diving attacks, enemies and an arena-wide strike that uses raised pillars as cover. Phase checkpoints preserve progress, while quality presets adjust expensive rendering features.

03 / the skills in the work

what this helped me practice.

The project develops boss-state design, procedural rendering and combat camera work. Performance tiers and shape-and-sound telegraphs add practical experience with scalability and accessibility.

  • Three.js
  • Boss encounter design
  • Procedural rendering
  • Combat cameras
  • Performance profiling

a note on the process.

The boss becomes easier to understand when its scale controls where and when it can be hurt. Shape and sound carry the warning alongside color.
