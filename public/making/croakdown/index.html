Croakdown — browser study

The earlier browser combat study: frog fighters, horde survival, and shared-screen co-op.

the curiositythe buildthe practice

01 / the question behind it

What happens when the player is both a frog fighter and the defender shaping the arena?

The earlier Croakdown browser study combines shared-screen frog combat with living swamp towers. TypeScript modules separate simulation, input, rendering and a feel layer driven by combat events.

02 / from idea to something playable

how the pieces came together.

  1. 01

    Separate rules from their presentation

    The simulation owns damage, knockback, enemy state and spawning. Rendering and the feel layer consume the results, so a landed hit can drive coordinated feedback.

  2. 02

    Make hits interact with the partner

    Launched enemies can be met by the other frog’s swing for a volley spike. Hitstop, launch thresholds and enemy armor rules give different attacks different consequences.

  3. 03

    Keep the prototype’s history visible

    The browser study remains a separate stage from the later native arena rebuild. Its role is to show the earlier combat and co-op direction, without presenting it as a current public release.

03 / the skills in the work

what this helped me practice.

The project develops event-based combat architecture, local co-op and procedural character rendering. It also explores how knockback and partner interactions can create decisions beyond dealing damage.

  • TypeScript
  • Canvas rendering
  • Local co-op
  • Combat events
  • Game feel

a note on the process.

A single authoritative hit event is a useful boundary: the simulation can stay consistent while sound, shake and animation all respond to the same impact.
